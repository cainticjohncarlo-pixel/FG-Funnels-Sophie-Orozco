# PART 3: ACCESS TESTING AND GOVERNANCE


Separation by architecture makes this short, but run it anyway. Never trust, always verify.


## Test accounts


- **TEST-STAFF:** fresh email, created as a contact, granted `STAFF - Setter` only.
- **TEST-CLIENT:** fresh email, run through your real client-facing flows (lead magnet opt-in, and if any client offer exists in FGF, grant that). This account should hold exactly what a real lead/client holds.


## The test battery


1. **TEST-STAFF portal check:** log in (incognito). Sees exactly three products: Start Here, Setter Onboarding, SOP Library. Cannot see Closer/Coach/CSM/VA courses. Check on desktop and the mobile app experience.
2. **TEST-STAFF privilege check:** confirm the account is a portal member only: no agency/sub-account login works with that email, no CRM access.
3. **TEST-CLIENT portal check:** attempt to log in to the staff portal with the client test email. Expected: no account / no access, or a portal showing only client-granted products with zero staff products visible.
4. **Offer exposure check:** for every `STAFF - ` offer, confirm no public checkout URL is live (try the raw checkout link logged out; it should be unpublished/disabled) and no funnel/page references it.
5. **Campaign bleed check:** confirm TEST-STAFF (tagged `staff`) is excluded from client campaigns by checking audience filters on active campaigns, and confirm TEST-CLIENT cannot receive a `staff-` tag from any active workflow (search workflow actions for `staff-` tags; only the grant workflows may contain them).
6. **Revoke check:** remove the offer from TEST-STAFF, confirm the login shows nothing / access is gone. This proves offboarding works before you ever need it.


All six pass → load content and go live. Log the test date in the access log.


## Governance


1. New course or lesson added → confirm it's inside an existing staff product, not a new public-facing product. New products get added to the role offers deliberately.
2. Quarterly: open each `STAFF - ` offer and review its member list against the current roster. Anyone who shouldn't be there is a same-day fix and an incident to understand.
3. Offboarding: revoke the offer(s), remove `staff-` tags, verify the login is dead (test 6 procedure), rotate any shared credentials, log it. Same day, every time.
4. The hub holds process, never client data, never credentials. Credentials live in the password manager only.

## Our Mission and Approach (written summary under Sophie's welcome video)


Sophie Orozco Coaching exists to save marriages that everyone else has given up on. Most of our clients come to us after therapy has already failed them. Many are fighting for their marriage alone, with a spouse who wants out or has already checked out.


Our approach is built on one core belief: a struggling marriage is not two broken people. It is two adapted selves stuck in a cycle of learned reactions, running on dysregulated nervous systems. Each spouse has started mistaking the cycle for the person they married. Nobody actually wants the version of the marriage they are in.


The work we do is helping one person return to their true self and become grounded. When that happens, the reactive cycle cannot survive. As Sophie puts it, darkness cannot survive in the presence of light. This is why our program works with just one willing spouse, even when the other wants nothing to do with coaching.


We have helped turn around more than 500 marriages. You will hear the phrase "true self" constantly here. Learn what it means, because it governs how we coach, how we sell, and how we talk to every single lead and client.

## Coaching Standards (written companion to the standards video)

We are coaches, not therapists, lawyers, or financial advisors.

- No legal advice on divorce, custody, or separation agreements. "That's a question for an attorney" is a complete sentence, delivered kindly.
- No financial planning advice.
- Faith and personal values: meet clients where they are. Never impose, never dismiss.

## What Setting Is at SOC and What Success Looks Like


You are Sophie in the DMs. Every message you send is written in first person as her, and it has to read like she typed it herself. Leads are coming to Sophie in the worst season of their marriage; the trust they extend to her name is the entire asset. Protect it.

## The SOC DM Framework (required reading)


The full framework document is attached to this lesson and is the operating manual for your role. The essentials it covers:


- **The conversation arc.** Every thread runs the full arc: open, real discovery, the cost of the situation, a matched proof resource, then an invitation. No shortcutting to the pitch, ever. We slow down on discovery and pull out the pain, the urgency, and the cost before we ever talk about a call.
- **We discover, we don't diagnose.** No confident "reads" on a lead who hasn't given us anything real yet. On a bare opener: a plain human beat and one discovery question. Insight-carrying reflections are earned by what the lead has actually shared.
- **The True Self lens.** Numbness, resentment, and "I don't feel anything" are the adapted self talking, never a disqualification. We separate the person from the cycle and open the possibility that anything is possible.
- **Resource before pitch.** Every lead gets a matched proof resource (testimonial or podcast episode, always the actual link) and engages with it before any invitation. "Listen and tell me what lands."
- **The invitation.** We reflect the lead's own words back and ask permission before pitching. The best sign a lead is ready: they articulate what the program would do for them in their own words.


## Conversation Guidelines: Tone and Discipline


- Warm, optimistic, hopeful. The lead should feel they came to exactly the right place. Confidence comes through short, certain lines, never long empathetic recaps.
- Short messages, 3 to 4 sentences max. One question per message. Contractions always. First name sparingly. No em dashes or en dashes, ever.
- No AI tells: no listing their words back, no stacked reflections, no polished formula. Brief human acknowledgment, then one clear question.
- Never chase. No double-texting when they stall, no over-explaining. We are in demand; the lead leans in.
- Lead things softer. Never open with a blunt refusal or negation, and never use hard-push phrasing like "so tell me straight." Warmth first, then the redirect.
- Never romantic, never flirtatious, with anyone. Married leads get total respect for their marriage.
- A lead's account of themselves is data. Their account of their spouse is one side of a story. Hold it that way for men and women alike.


## Lead Qualification and the Green/Yellow/Red Lens


- **Green:** acute crisis (spouse just left, divorce papers moving). Pre-sold pain, move with pace and care.
- **Yellow:** chronic disconnection (roommate marriage, slow drift). Real fit, but urgency has to be surfaced through discovery before an invitation lands.
- **Red:** not a fit right now (no committed relationship to save, free-therapy seeking with no ownership, or a safety situation, which follows the crisis protocol instead).


Two rules that govern all scoring: hopeless language is never a red light, and a vague or resigned answer ("I've kind of accepted it") is not a decision to leave their marriage. That is exhaustion talking. Keep discovering: ask what the resignation is about and what they'd want if it cleared. Only route someone toward an exit once they have genuinely told us they want out.


Money: no price anchoring in DMs, and no heavy financial pre-frames (they read as bracing for impact). The application gates finances. Your job is light vetting through natural discovery, surfacing whether they are the decision-maker and whether investing in the marriage is on the table for them.

## Crisis Protocol


Stop the normal sequence immediately, no gates, no pitch, if a lead:


- States suicidal intent, a plan, or means.
- Says they are afraid of their spouse.
- Discloses that a child is being harmed or is unsafe. Child safety overrides everything: make sure the child is safe now, gently learn what's happening, and steer toward appropriate help.


Be a human first: one genuine, caring beat, encourage the right kind of help, and flag the thread to Chris immediately. Raw grief (a death, a miscarriage, just-discovered infidelity) also gets met as a person first, one genuine human beat, before any framework step. But note the threshold: volatility, jealousy, accusations, and eggshells language are painful marriage dynamics we coach, not crises. Don't over-read two words into a psychological event, and don't go to alert posture for hyperbolic frustration.

- **Keep nurturing** when belief isn't built yet: they haven't engaged the resource, can't say what they want, or the urgency isn't real to them yet. A booked lead who isn't ready is a wasted closer call and a burned lead.
- **Flag to Chris** anything ambiguous, any crisis-protocol thread, and any lead asking questions you can't answer in Sophie's voice. Chris is the conductor and can override how any thread is handled.


## Follow-Up: Walkaway Sequence and Lost Leads

- One willing spouse is enough. We never treat an uninvolved or resistant spouse as a reason a client cannot succeed.
- A client's account of themselves is data. A client's account of their spouse is one side of a story. Hold both with care, and apply that standard evenly to husbands and wives.
- Numbness, hopelessness, and "I feel nothing" are the adapted self talking. Meet them with warmth and confidence, never alarm.
- We discover, we don't lecture. Guide clients to their own realizations through questions with insight woven in. Abrupt corrections don't serve discovery, even when a client voices a harmful self-belief.


## Boundaries: What Coaches Do and Don't Advise On


We are coaches, not therapists, lawyers, or financial advisors.


- No clinical diagnosis, no medication advice, no treatment recommendations. If a client is working with a therapist or doctor, we complement that care, we never countermand it.
- No legal advice on divorce, custody, or separation agreements. "That's a question for an attorney" is a complete sentence, delivered kindly.
- No financial planning advice.
- Faith and personal values: meet clients where they are. Never impose, never dismiss.

## What Setting Is at SOC and What Success Looks Like


You are Sophie in the DMs. Every message you send is written in first person as her, and it has to read like she typed it herself. Leads are coming to Sophie in the worst season of their marriage; the trust they extend to her name is the entire asset. Protect it.


Success is not maximum bookings. Success is qualified, pre-sold leads arriving at calls already believing this is the answer, having consumed our proof content, and having said in their own words why they want it. One great conversation that books ready is worth five rushed ones that no-show or arrive cold.


## Lead Sources


Conversations come from: Sophie's organic content (Instagram is the dominant channel, plus TikTok and Facebook), paid Meta messaging ads, and CJ/affiliate audiences arriving warm. Ad leads generally run colder than organic and CJ leads: expect to do more discovery and belief-building, not less.


## The SOC DM Framework (required reading)


The full framework document is attached to this lesson and is the operating manual for your role. The essentials it covers:


- **The conversation arc.** Every thread runs the full arc: open, real discovery, the cost of the situation, a matched proof resource, then an invitation. No shortcutting to the pitch, ever. We slow down on discovery and pull out the pain, the urgency, and the cost before we ever talk about a call.
- **We discover, we don't diagnose.** No confident "reads" on a lead who hasn't given us anything real yet. On a bare opener: a plain human beat and one discovery question. Insight-carrying reflections are earned by what the lead has actually shared.
- **The True Self lens.** Numbness, resentment, and "I don't feel anything" are the adapted self talking, never a disqualification. We separate the person from the cycle and open the possibility that anything is possible.
- **Resource before pitch.** Every lead gets a matched proof resource (testimonial or podcast episode, always the actual link) and engages with it before any invitation. "Listen and tell me what lands."
- **The invitation.** We reflect the lead's own words back and ask permission before pitching. The best sign a lead is ready: they articulate what the program would do for them in their own words.


## Conversation Guidelines: Tone and Discipline


- Warm, optimistic, hopeful. The lead should feel they came to exactly the right place. Confidence comes through short, certain lines, never long empathetic recaps.
- Short messages, 3 to 4 sentences max. One question per message. Contractions always. First name sparingly. No em dashes or en dashes, ever.
- No AI tells: no listing their words back, no stacked reflections, no polished formula. Brief human acknowledgment, then one clear question.
- Never chase. No double-texting when they stall, no over-explaining. We are in demand; the lead leans in.
- Lead things softer. Never open with a blunt refusal or negation, and never use hard-push phrasing like "so tell me straight." Warmth first, then the redirect.
- Never romantic, never flirtatious, with anyone. Married leads get total respect for their marriage.
- A lead's account of themselves is data. Their account of their spouse is one side of a story. Hold it that way for men and women alike.


## Lead Qualification and the Green/Yellow/Red Lens


- **Green:** acute crisis (spouse just left, divorce papers moving). Pre-sold pain, move with pace and care.
- **Yellow:** chronic disconnection (roommate marriage, slow drift). Real fit, but urgency has to be surfaced through discovery before an invitation lands.
- **Red:** not a fit right now (no committed relationship to save, free-therapy seeking with no ownership, or a safety situation, which follows the crisis protocol instead).


Two rules that govern all scoring: hopeless language is never a red light, and a vague or resigned answer ("I've kind of accepted it") is not a decision to leave their marriage. That is exhaustion talking. Keep discovering: ask what the resignation is about and what they'd want if it cleared. Only route someone toward an exit once they have genuinely told us they want out.


Money: no price anchoring in DMs, and no heavy financial pre-frames (they read as bracing for impact). The application gates finances. Your job is light vetting through natural discovery, surfacing whether they are the decision-maker and whether investing in the marriage is on the table for them.

Doors stay open; people's situations change.


## Crisis Protocol


Stop the normal sequence immediately, no gates, no pitch, if a lead:


- States suicidal intent, a plan, or means.
- Says they are afraid of their spouse.
- Discloses that a child is being harmed or is unsafe. Child safety overrides everything: make sure the child is safe now, gently learn what's happening, and steer toward appropriate help.


Be a human first: one genuine, caring beat, encourage the right kind of help, and flag the thread to Chris immediately. Raw grief (a death, a miscarriage, just-discovered infidelity) also gets met as a person first, one genuine human beat, before any framework step. But note the threshold: volatility, jealousy, accusations, and eggshells language are painful marriage dynamics we coach, not crises. Don't over-read two words into a psychological event, and don't go to alert posture for hyperbolic frustration.


## When to Pass to a Closer vs Keep Nurturing


- **Book it** when the arc is complete: discovery done, cost surfaced, resource consumed and discussed, fit articulated in their own words, finances not an obvious wall.
- **Keep nurturing** when belief isn't built yet: they haven't engaged the resource, can't say what they want, or the urgency isn't real to them yet. A booked lead who isn't ready is a wasted closer call and a burned lead.
- **Flag to Chris** anything ambiguous, any crisis-protocol thread, and any lead asking questions you can't answer in Sophie's voice. Chris is the conductor and can override how any thread is handled.


## Follow-Up: Walkaway Sequence and Lost Leads


- Threads that stall after real engagement enter the **Walkaway Sequence**: spaced, warm, no-pressure touches that leave the door open without chasing. The sequence document is attached to this lesson.
- Older dead threads are worked through the **Lost Lead Follow-Up System**. Situations change fast in struggling marriages, and a lead who ghosted in March may be in crisis in June. Re-opens start with genuine warmth and a real question, never "just following up."
- Log every thread's status in the tracker so follow-ups are never doubled and no lead falls through.
