When writing outreach copy (LinkedIn DMs, connect notes, follow-ups) for the user:

1. **No em dashes (—) or en dashes (–) in message bodies.** Replace with commas, periods, or restructure. Hyphens in compound words (follow-up, no-shows, 30-minute, consult-to-start) are fine.
2. **Never use the phrase "sales pitch"** — it primes the reader to expect one. Reframe as "quick chat", "working conversation", "no pressure", or just omit.
3. **Short and concise wins.** Cut throat-clearing context, redundant qualifiers, and meta-commentary. If a sentence does not change what the reader does next, delete it.
4. **Natural tone over polished tone.** AI-feeling cues to avoid: leading em dash after greeting (`Hi [Name] —`), "really impressed", "would love to", over-formal closings.

**Why:** The user is sending these manually one at a time and reads as the prospect would. AI-feeling copy gets ignored or flagged. Concise messages also fit LinkedIn's connect-note 300-char limit without juggling.

**How to apply:** Apply the no-em/en-dash rule to ANY client-facing deliverable I produce for this user, not just outreach: LinkedIn DMs, email templates, cold copy, AND documents, PDFs, presentations/decks, and deployed web pages (e.g. Vercel pages). On 2026-07-09 the user asked to strip all em dashes from a deployed flowchart page saying it "will not look like AI." Treat em/en dashes as a default-off across everything customer- or client-facing. Hyphens in compound words remain fine. (Supersedes the earlier note that em dashes were acceptable in docs/presentations.)
